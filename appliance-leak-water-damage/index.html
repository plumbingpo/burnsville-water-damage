Appliance Leak Water Damage in Burnsville: What to Know

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

On every Burnsville job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • The number of rooms affected, counting a ceiling under a laundry on an upper floor.
  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • If the flooring and cabinets have to come out or can be dried where they are.
